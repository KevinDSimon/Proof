# 0025. Perspective correction and Upright

Date: 2026-11-11
Status: Accepted

## Context
Issues #80 and #81 add Lightroom's Transform panel: manual perspective sliders, and Upright, which sets them from straight lines in the photo. Buildings photographed from below lean back, because their upright sides converge toward the top of the frame; this correction makes them parallel. Vision finds horizons (`DetectHorizonRequest`, used by the crop tool's Auto) and contours, but no straight line segments, so Upright needs its own way to find them.

## Decision
- The correction is a homography: the photo is turned as if the camera had been aimed differently, `K R K⁻¹`, where `R` tilts about the horizontal axis (Vertical), then about the vertical axis (Horizontal), then turns in the image plane (Rotate). `K` assumes a focal length equal to the photo's diagonal, the field of view of a 43 mm lens on a full-frame camera. The real focal length doesn't change whether lines come out parallel, only how much the corrected photo is stretched, which Aspect undoes; a fixed value keeps the render independent of what the camera recorded, which manual lenses often don't. Vertical and Horizontal run from −100 to 100, for up to 45° of tilt; Rotate from −10° to 10°; Aspect from −100 to 100, up to a √2 stretch each way; Scale from 50 to 150 percent; X and Y Offset from −100 to 100, up to a quarter of the photo. Negative Vertical widens the top, as in Lightroom. The ranges are chosen, not matched value for value against Lightroom.
- After the tilt, the photo's center is moved back where it was, so a tilt doesn't slide the photo off the frame.
- Constrain Crop, on by default, enlarges and moves the corrected photo so the largest rectangle of the frame's shape inside it fills the frame. The rectangle is found exactly: its center and size are three unknowns under sixteen linear limits (each of its corners inside each edge of the corrected photo), and every corner of that small problem is tried. Off shows the empty edges, clear on the canvas. Distortion, which always fills the frame (decision 0024), has no such switch because its lost edges are small; a perspective correction can lose a third of the photo, so the choice matters here.
- The stage runs right after manual distortion, on the whole photo as the camera oriented it, and before the crop's quarter turns. Lines are then straight before they are measured, and the correction stays with the photo when it is turned. It uses `CIPerspectiveTransform` with the four corners the homography gives.
- Line segments are found by the region-growing step of LSD (von Gioi et al., 2012) on a 1024-pixel, gamma-encoded render with white balance, detail and lens corrections but no perspective, crop or tone. Neighboring pixels whose edges run within 22.5° of each other are grouped, strongest edges first, and a group becomes a segment when it is at least 6% of the long side, its edges run along it, and it fills at least half its bounding rectangle. LSD's statistical test is left out: Upright needs a building's long edges, not every faint line.
- Upright takes segments within 25° of upright or level and solves for the sliders by Gauss–Newton, minimizing how far each corrected segment leans off upright or level, weighted by length. Four rounds of reweighting let lines that stay well off, such as a roof's slope, count for little. Level solves Rotate only; Vertical adds Vertical; Full adds Horizontal; Auto solves like Full, adds Horizontal only when level lines make up at least a fifth of the length, then corrects tilts beyond 20 only by 60% for the rest, since a steep correction looks stretched. Guided straightens up to four drawn guides exactly, solving each tilt only when two guides pin it.
- Upright writes ordinary slider values, which stay editable, and records its mode for the buttons. The crop's straighten angle goes back to zero, since Upright levels the photo itself. Guides are kept in the edit, in fractions of the uncorrected photo, so they can be changed later.
- Guided shows the whole photo, uncropped and unturned, with zoom off, the space guides are stored in. Guides are drawn where they fall on the corrected photo, and each new guide corrects the photo at once.
- The card gets a switch (`AdjustmentGroup.transform`) and starts off unless the edit has a correction, as Vignette does. A missing `transform` is no correction, so `processVersion` stays at 1.

## Consequences
- Upright finds nothing in photos without long straight edges, such as landscapes, and says so; Guided still works there.
- The 43 mm assumption leaves wide-angle corrections slightly stretched upward; Aspect corrects it by eye. Reading the focal length from the file could set Aspect automatically later.
- Line finding takes about a second on a 60 MP photo, most of it the render; the segments are kept while the lens corrections stay the same, so trying each mode is immediate.
- Strong corrections enlarge the photo and lose resolution at its edges, as in Lightroom.
