# 0040. Masks

Date: 2026-11-16
Status: Accepted

## Context
Issue #27 adds masks: adjustments that apply to part of the photo, such as darkening a sky the photo was exposed for and lifting the foreground. It covers linear and radial gradients, a brush, and the photo's subject found by Vision, in one change. Masks limited by brightness or color, and masks combined from several shapes, are left for #190. The recipe has had an empty `layers` field since the first format so that masks wouldn't need a format change.

## Decision
- Each layer is one mask and its own adjustments: Temperature, Tint, Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites, Blacks, Texture, Clarity, Dehaze and Saturation, the local sliders a landscape edit uses most. Sharpness, noise reduction and hue are left out.
- Layers apply after the global tone and before color: step 3 of the pipeline, where the plan had them after the look. They work on the same linear light the global tone sliders do, and the color sliders and the look apply on top, so a look doesn't need redoing per mask. Each layer runs its adjustments on the whole photo through the global stages (`HighlightsShadows`, `TextureClarity`, `Dehaze`, `ToneLookup`, `ColorAdjustments`), then blends the result in by its mask with one Metal kernel. Layers apply in order, each to the result of the one before.
- Temperature and Tint are relative, from −100 to +100, not Kelvin: the photo's white balance is already set, and a mask shifts it. They scale red against blue by up to half a stop, and green by up to two fifths of a stop, normalized so a gray keeps its brightness. They are a channel scale through `CIColorMatrix`, combined with Exposure.
- Masks are placed like spots (decision 0039): from 0 to 1 of the whole photo after spot removal and before the crop, so a mask stays on what it covers when the crop changes. The tool maps clicks through the crop with `Crop.photoPoint` and `resultPoint`.
- A linear gradient is two points: full effect on the start's side of the line through the start, none past the line through the end, eased with `smoothstep`. A radial gradient is a center, two radii as fractions of the long edge so a circle stays round, an angle and a feather. Both are Metal generator kernels, so they cost nothing to store and are exact at every size.
- A brush mask is its strokes: each a path, radius, feather, flow and whether it erases. They are drawn on the CPU into a grayscale image 2048 pixels on its long edge, the same at every render size, and the last few are kept. Each stroke is drawn as the most its brush reached each pixel, so going back and forth within one stroke doesn't build up; then it adds to the mask by its flow, or takes away from it.
- A subject mask is found once, with Vision's foreground instance mask (`VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest`, every instance together), on the whole uncropped photo with its global adjustments, at 1536 pixels on the long edge. The mask is stored in the sidecar as a grayscale PNG, about 30 KB. Finding it again at each render would cost about half a second and could change when Vision changes, which would change how a saved edit renders.
- Any mask can be inverted. A kind of mask this version doesn't know covers nothing, and its type is written back as it was.
- The tool opens from the toolbar or Photo › Masks (M). Its controls take the inspector's place, as Spot Removal's do: a Masking card with a New section, a button for each kind, above a Masks section listing the masks already made, each with a switch to hide its adjustments, and Show Overlay (O); then the selected mask's shape controls and Invert, and its adjustments. A new gradient appears at once where it is most often wanted, a linear one fading down from the top and a radial one in the middle, so adding one always shows something to work with; it is then moved and shaped on the photo. Every mask has a pin on the photo that selects it; a gradient's or brush mask's pin also moves it. A linear gradient turns by its middle line and changes its fade by its outer lines; a radial one moves from inside, sizes by its side handles and turns by the handle above it. Each handle and line has the pointer for what dragging it does, and the canvas's open hand is hidden while the tool is open.
- The selected mask is tinted red while Show Overlay is on, and while all its adjustments are 0, so a new mask can be seen as it is placed. Moving one of its sliders turns Show Overlay off, so the change can be seen. The overlay is drawn by the renderer, after the look and before the crop, from `EditRecipe.maskOverlay`, which is not saved.
- While a brush stroke is painted, only its newest part is drawn: the mask before the stroke and the stroke's own reach are kept, so each step costs about the area the brush just covered rather than every stroke again.
- An edit without layers renders as before, so `processVersion` stays 1. No version of Proof wrote a layer before, so the change to the layer's fields needs no migration; a layer in the old form reads as covering nothing.

## Consequences
- A brush mask on a 60 MP export is drawn at 2048 pixels and enlarged, so a hard-edged brush has an edge about a dozen photo pixels wide at full size. Feathered brushes don't show it.
- Masks don't follow lens corrections or perspective changed after they are drawn, as spots don't.
- A subject mask doesn't update when the photo's global adjustments change; adding it again finds the subject anew.
- Masks is an item in Copy Settings, unticked by default, since masks are drawn around what one photo shows. Pasting replaces the target's layers.
