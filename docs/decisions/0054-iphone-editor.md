# 0054. The iPhone editor: five tools in native sheets

Date: 2026-11-26
Status: Accepted. Replaces 0048's "Edit on iPhone" and the iPhone inspector sheet (#228).

## Context
Issue #285. Decision 0048 gave the iPhone the Mac's inspector, every card in the Mac's order, in a sheet below the photo (#228). Decision 0051 then made iPad and iPhone serve a mobile workflow: quick edits on the go, detailed editing back on the Mac. A phone showing curves, selective color, lens corrections and transform in a small sheet is a mobile Lightroom, which is the wrong tool for that workflow. Apple Photos, VSCO and Apple Maps show a better shape for the phone: a few focused tools, each in a sheet that leaves the photo in view.

## Decision
- **iPhone only.** The iPhone (`UIDevice.userInterfaceIdiom == .phone`) gets `PhoneEditor` in the iOS app, reached from the Edit button of the photo selected in the grid since #292. iPad keeps `PhotoView`, its trailing inspector and its keyboard shortcuts, including in a narrow window.
- **Navigation bar.** The standard Back button, the filename as the title, Share, and a More menu. Compare shows "Original" as the bar's subtitle.
- **More** is a native `Menu`: Undo, Redo and History; Copy Edits and Paste Edits; Compare and Histogram as toggles; and Reset, which reverts the photo as Revert to Original does, without asking, since Undo brings it back. Reset isn't drawn as destructive, so it stays white.
- **Bottom toolbar.** Five `ToolbarItem`s with flexible `ToolbarSpacer`s between them, so each is its own Liquid Glass button: Looks, Light, Color, Finish and Crop. No segmented control or custom bar.
- **Tool sheets.** Looks, Light, Color and Finish each open a sheet with a compact height (212 points: the title and two sliders, with room above the first) and the system's medium height; in landscape only the compact one. The sheet has the tool's name, Reset for that tool's sliders and a close button, with the system's soft scroll edge under that bar. The photo is inset by the sheet's height, measured from its top edge, as the #228 sheet did, so it stays whole above the sheet at both heights, and stays zoomable while the sheet is up. Sliders are `AdjustmentSlider`, which is a SwiftUI `Slider` with the inspector's steps and track colors, and render live during a drag through the same path as the inspector.
  - Looks: the imported LUTs and Strength (`LookSection` without its card). There are no built-in looks.
  - Light: Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks.
  - Color: a White Balance row, then Temperature, Tint, Saturation and Vibrance (see White balance below).
  - Finish: Grain, Clarity, Sharpness and Vignette, each the amount only. Fade is left out.
- **Sheet colors.** The tool sheets are opaque, not glass, in the system's `tertiarySystemBackground`. In the app's dark interface that is about 18% gray: a step lighter than the 13% canvas, so the sheet stands apart from it, and close to the gray of the inspector cards on the Mac and iPad. The touch tools' sheets (crop, and spot removal and masks from a keyboard) keep the iPad inspector's colors, cards on the canvas gray, and History keeps its own panel's.
- **Histogram.** Turned on from More, it floats in a small container in the same gray as the tool sheets, over the canvas's top-leading corner, 48 points tall rather than the inspector's 84. It is temporary and small, so it may sit over the canvas, as an on-image tool does.
- **Crop** is a mode of the canvas, not a sheet (#287). The touch crop from #229 stays on the photo, the same `Crop` model and engine as the Mac and iPad, and the rule-of-thirds grid shows the whole time rather than only during a drag, as in Photos, so the photo can be framed before touching it; Straighten's finer grid still replaces it while that slider moves. Straighten, an `AdjustmentSlider` as on the Mac, sits below the photo. The bottom toolbar's five tools give way to Rotate (a quarter turn counterclockwise), Flip (left to right), Aspect Ratio and Reset, each its own Liquid Glass button; the bar's Back, Share and More give way to Cancel and Done, with "Crop" as the title, so the mode is left only one of those two ways. Done keeps the crop, saved like any other edit, and Cancel returns to the edit from when the mode opened, as on the Mac. Aspect Ratio is a native `Menu` with a picker: Freeform and Original, then 1:1, 4:5, 5:7, 3:2 and 16:9, then Landscape or Portrait to swap the rectangle. The phone leaves out custom ratios, Draw Line, Auto and Flip Vertical: typed ratios and a drawn level line want a keyboard and precision the Mac and iPad give, and these are rarely needed on the go. A custom ratio made on the Mac shows as Custom, checked, and stays until another is picked. The histogram hides while cropping, since it would sit over the crop handles.
- **White balance** (#298). A row above Temperature and Tint in the Color sheet holds one native menu: the presets as a picker, in the Mac's order (As Shot, Auto, Daylight, Cloudy, Shade, Tungsten, Fluorescent, Flash), each with a symbol for its light, then Eyedropper in a section of its own. A separate eyedropper button beside the menu was tried and dropped: two controls crowded the row and pushed Tint below the compact height. The menu lists and shows Custom once a slider has moved, as on the Mac, and choosing a preset switches White Balance on, as a slider does. The row has the same space below it as a slider's track gives the next slider. The eyedropper is a mode of the canvas, like Crop: the sheet lowers and a loupe, the same `LoupeView` as the iPad's, sits on the photo, starting at its center, with a ring around the measured point. Dragging the loupe or the ring moves it, keeping it clear of the finger; a drag elsewhere or a tap moves it to the finger, and two fingers zoom and pan. Temperature and Tint follow each spot as it is measured, by the eyedropper's search from decision 0019 through `WhiteBalanceSampler`, so the photo shows the result before it is kept. Nothing is measured until the loupe first moves, since the center of a photo is often not neutral, so opening the mode changes nothing by itself. While a spot is measured, the next one waits, and only the latest is measured after it. Nothing sits below the photo, so it fills the space the sheet left; the loupe's values and the photo itself show the result. The bar's Back, Share and More give way to Cancel and a checkmark, with "White Balance" as the title. The checkmark keeps the white balance as one undo step, waiting for a spot still being measured, and Cancel returns to the white balance, preset and switch from when the mode opened (`WhiteBalanceSampling`); the sheet then comes back. A spot too dark to tell its color leaves the last one's white balance. The iPad picks when a finger lifts instead, since there the finger leaves more of the photo in view; on a phone the loupe stays and the person confirms.
- **Hidden tools.** Spot removal, masks and Guided Upright stay in the code and in the recipe but have no control on iPhone. #288 brings spot removal and masks back under More. A keyboard's shortcuts can still open Spot Removal and Masks, so their controls and Done still show when that happens.
- **Model.** `PhoneTool` and `PhoneAdjustment` in `ProofKit` (with `PhoneWhiteBalance` and `WhiteBalanceSampling` for the white balance row and eyedropper mode) list each tool's sliders and read, write and reset one recipe value each, so they're tested without a view. They write only their own value: curves, HSL and selective color, black and white, noise reduction, masks, spots, lens, transform, crop and the other effects settings are kept as they are. The sheets have no card switches, so moving a slider in a card switched off on the Mac (`switchedOff`) switches that card on first, as choosing a look already did. Nothing about rendering or the recipe format changes.

## Consequences
- An edit made on the Mac with tools the iPhone lacks still renders on the iPhone and survives editing there.
- The iPhone has fewer controls than before: Auto Tone, Texture, Dehaze, the curve and the rest are Mac and iPad only. Those edits render on iPhone but can only be changed elsewhere.
- `PhoneToolSliders` in `ProofUI` is used only by the iPhone. It lives in `ProofUI` because the inspector's track colors are there.
- Since #286 the editor has no filmstrip, so the photo has the room.

## Reaching the editor (#292)
Decision 0055 put a photo viewer with a filmstrip between the grid and the editor (#286). After trying it, the viewer added a screen without adding much: rating, Info and Share don't need the photo full screen, and moving between photos is quicker in the grid. #292 replaces it:
- **A tap in the iPhone grid selects the photo** instead of opening it, marked with the rounded selection outline, and VoiceOver reads it as selected. Tapping it again, or an empty part of the grid, deselects it. A tap on another photo moves the selection to it. The rule is `BrowseState.tapToSelect` in `ProofKit`; the iOS app turns it on through `LibraryPlatform.tapSelectsPhoto`, set for `userInterfaceIdiom == .phone`, so `ProofUI` stays free of UIKit.
- **Bottom toolbar.** While a photo is selected, the grid's bottom toolbar has Share, Rating, Info and Edit: four `ToolbarItem`s with flexible `ToolbarSpacer`s between them, each its own system Liquid Glass button, in the same order as the viewer had. Share opens the export sheet (#234). Rating is a native `Menu` with the inline rating picker, written to the photo's XMP sidecar; its star fills when the photo has a rating, and VoiceOver reads the stars as its value. Info opens `PhotoInfoSheet`. Edit pushes `PhoneEditor`, and Back returns to the grid with the photo still selected. Share and Edit are off for JPEG and HEIC photos until #239; they can still be rated and get Info.
- **Select** is unchanged: it starts with nothing selected and has its own bottom toolbar of Share, Rating, Flag and Paste Settings. The top bar's More menu stays as it was.
- **Removed.** `PhoneViewer` is gone. The shared `Filmstrip` stays, since the Mac and iPad use it.
- **iPad** still opens the photo with a tap.
